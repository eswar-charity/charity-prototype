1. Executive Summary
Charity Hub is being shaped as a social participation network for nonprofit storytelling, creative event-led engagement, and voluntary giving. The product should not be positioned narrowly as a donation page or fundraising form. Its strategic center is SE-led and nonprofit-led content, storytelling, and event participation, where donations become a natural by-product of engagement rather than the first product ask.
The product aims to make nonprofits culturally engaging, social, and participatory. The landing experience should feel like a living content feed of nonprofit stories, creative events, community moments, and engagement opportunities. Users should discover, feel, engage, and participate before being asked to donate.
Competition For Good is the named product engagement model that expresses this direction. It turns nonprofit support into a friendly, purpose-driven social participation loop where Social Entrepreneurs, nonprofits, communities, teams, creators, and supporters compete creatively through stories, events, challenges, content, and participation. The goal is not to make giving feel like a financial transaction first; the goal is to make nonprofit causes culturally engaging enough that users want to watch, join, share, create, and voluntarily give.
Product Positioning
Charity Hub is a social participation network where verified nonprofits and Social Entrepreneurs turn creative events, storytelling, and community participation into fundraising momentum and measurable impact.

2. Product Thesis and North Star
2.1 Product Thesis
Core Thesis
Any event can become a reason to raise money - when the event is meaningful, creative, social, and emotionally engaging enough for people to participate first.
 |
The product thesis is rooted in the belief that nonprofit engagement is currently too donation-centric, transactional, formal, and often emotionally underpowered. Charity Hub seeks to change that by turning causes into dynamic content-led participation experiences.
Competition For Good should be treated as the core engagement expression of the thesis: creative events and storytelling create attention, participation, and community energy; donations follow as the by-product of emotional connection and social momentum.

2.3 What Charity Hub Is Not
Not just a donation collection platform.
Not a generic peer-to-peer campaign form.
Not a campaign directory sorted by amount raised.
Not a template-driven event autofill product that limits creativity.
Not a payment-first user experience where the main emotion is obligation.

8. Entity Lifecycle Models
Entity
 | Lifecycle States
 | Key Rule / Note
 |
Nonprofit
 | Draft -> Submitted -> Pending Verification -> Verified -> Active -> Rejected -> Suspended
 | EIN must resolve against IRS, GuideStar, FDO, or Charity Navigator. No manual fallback unless policy changes.
 |
Social Entrepreneur
 | Anonymous -> Triggered -> Registered -> Profile Complete -> Active -> Restricted -> Suspended
 | Adult SEs follow normal identity and profile completion rules. Minor SEs are included only through a parent/guardian consent model with action-level controls.
 |
Event
 | Draft -> Submitted -> Changes Requested -> Approved -> System Auto-Published -> Live -> Completed -> Archived / Cancelled / Rejected
 | Nonprofit-created events auto-publish after creation if prerequisites are met. SE-created events auto-publish after nonprofit approval.
 |
Donation
 | Initiated -> Pending -> Successful -> Failed -> Refunded -> Disputed
 | Donation requires identity/payment capture and settlement-ready 501(c)(3).
 |
Payout
 | Not Ready -> Pending Setup -> Payout Pending -> Processing -> Paid -> Failed -> Reconciled
 | Actual payout, payout evidence, and reconciliation must be documented as full operating journey.
 |
Content / Engagement
 | Created -> Published -> Flagged -> Under Review -> Hidden -> Removed
 | Nonprofit Admin moderates event content. Anonymous users can view/share only.
 |
Admin Review Item
 | New -> In Review -> Approved / Rejected / Escalated -> Closed
 | Used for nonprofit verification, exceptions, escalations, and operational controls.
 |
Social Entrepreneur - Minor Age-Control State
 | Anonymous Discovery -> Minor Indicated -> Parent/Guardian Consent Pending -> Consent Verified -> Controlled Active -> Restricted -> Suspended
 | This is an age-based control path inside the SE lifecycle, not a separate persona. Every meaningful action requires the applicable parent/guardian consent/control path.

 | Make creator identity, recognition, content, and social impact visible.

 | Without compelling feed/content, donation by-product will not happen.
 | High
 | Prioritize content feed, story formats, participation loops, and creative event expression.
 |

 | Nonprofit Admin may be overwhelmed moderating content.
 | Medium
 | Design clear moderation queues, flags, user messages, and escalation paths.
 |

 | Donation, receipt, payout, reconciliation, disputes add operational risk.
 | High
 | Separate payment/payout journeys, define owner, status states, and legal review.

 | High
 | Assign founder/legal ownership and avoid undocumented assumptions.
 |
AI Overclaiming
 | AI could be perceived as autonomous trust/compliance decisioning.
 | Medium

Clarification Area
 | Founder-Aligned Position
 | Product Implication
 |
Product Category
 | Charity Hub should be positioned as a social participation network for nonprofit storytelling, creative events, and engagement-led giving, not primarily as a donation platform.
 | The product experience should lead with participation, storytelling, content, and community engagement. Donation remains important but should be treated as an outcome of engagement rather than the product’s primary identity.
 |
Landing Page Experience
 | The landing page should be a content-driven feed, not a donation/campaign directory.
 | The first user impression should create curiosity, emotion, and cultural energy. Users should feel they are entering a living feed of nonprofit stories, creative events, Social Entrepreneur activity, and community participation.
 |
Donation Visual Hierarchy
 | Donation should be de-emphasized as the first ask, but not hidden or made confusing.
 | The primary hierarchy should lead with story, event, participation, and engagement. Donation should appear as a natural next action after the user emotionally connects with the cause or event.
 |
Blank Canvas Event Creation
 | Event creation should provide high creative freedom because the blank canvas is central to Charity Hub’s moat.
 | The product should avoid rigid donation-first templates, pre-filled campaign structures, and generic fundraising forms. Guardrails should apply only where needed for nonprofit brand safety, compliance, harmful content, donation integrity, and approval workflows.
 |
Core Feed / Content Experience
 | The minimum feed experience should make nonprofit activity feel culturally engaging and socially alive.
 | The feed should include event stories, creator-led posts, nonprofit moments, participation prompts, visual content, social proof, engagement actions, and clear pathways into events. The product should feel like “what’s happening for good right now,” not “who is asking for money.”
 |
Competition For Good
 | Charity Hub’s engagement model should be framed as Competition For Good: purpose-led, creative, social participation where people, communities, and Social Entrepreneurs generate awareness, engagement, and donations through events.
 | Competition For Good should become a core product concept that supports challenges, event participation, storytelling, creative contribution, and voluntary giving. It should be positive, social, and mission-led rather than transactional.
 |
Monetization Direction
 | Monetization should be explored through an engagement-led lens.
 | Short term, transaction/payment economics may be easiest to validate. Medium term, nonprofit subscription/Launchpad value and data/analytics should be explored. Long term, ad revenue and merchant services become stronger once engagement volume, audience attention, and nonprofit community value are proven.
